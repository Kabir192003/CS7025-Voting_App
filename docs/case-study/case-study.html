01 · Summary

From a working prototype to a product with a point of view

This or That lets people post a decision they are stuck on and have the community vote. The first version worked, but it looked generic, showed placeholder content, and had security problems serious enough to block a public launch. This study covers the audit, the research behind each design decision, the new visual system and the plan for the backend.

9problem areas found in the audit, 4 of them critical
6peer-reviewed or primary sources shaped the design
7screens designed, desktop and mobile

The three decisions that define the redesign

  • Vote, then reveal. Results stay hidden until you vote. Three experiments show that visible tallies pull later votes toward the leader.
  • Strictly two sides. One question, two options, shown as a single split bar with a 50% line. Every result reads at a glance.
  • A results-desk vocabulary. Polls are called like elections: too early, too close, leaning, landslide. Each label maps to a rule on the server.

What changed for users

  • Voting is one tap with no page reload, and the split animates in.
  • Arguments are grouped by side, so you read the strongest case for each option.
  • Askers report what they chose in the end, which closes the loop for voters.
  • A profile shows your record: how often you side with the crowd, by topic.
  • A live check helps people write better questions before posting.

02 · Audit of the existing app

What was there

I read every file in the codebase: 6 controllers, 6 route files, 7 pages, a 2,223-line stylesheet, the schema and the seed scripts. The backend structure was sound. The problems were in configuration, data integrity and the front end.

CRITICAL
Production database password committed to the repo. It was base64-encoded as a fallback in config/db.js, beside a hard-coded JWT secret. Anyone with the repo could read the database or forge a login. OWASP A02 Security Misconfiguration.
CRITICAL
Stored cross-site scripting. Usernames and avatar data reached innerHTML and an <img src> attribute without escaping. OWASP A05 Injection.
CRITICAL
Schema out of sync with the code. schema.sql lacked seven columns the code reads, including email and display name, so a fresh install failed at sign-up.
CRITICAL
Topics that silently did nothing. The interface offered 16 topics, but the database held 6 differently named ones. Most picks were dropped without an error, so personalisation rarely worked.
HIGH
Votes and comments shared one row. A unique key on (question, user) meant each person could leave only one comment, and a new one overwrote the old. Votes also weren't checked against the question, so an option from another poll was accepted. OWASP A01 Broken Access Control.
HIGH
Fake content in the live UI. The hero (“1,240 active votes”), leaderboard, trending topics and profile defaults (“Kelly”, ID TT-2025-00421) were all hard-coded.
HIGH
Feeds that didn't do what they said. “New” called the same endpoint as “For you”. “Trending” sorted by all-time totals. “Unanswered” sorted by fewest votes, so answered polls still appeared.
MEDIUM
No hardening. No rate limiting on login, no security headers (although the README said there were), a 10 MB request limit, and a full page reload after every vote.
MEDIUM
Dead controls. Save buttons, the question “type” field and the comments-off setting were stored or drawn but never acted on.

03 · Research · how crowds behave

Showing the score changes the score

The old app showed live percentages before you voted. The research on social influence says that design is the main threat to a poll's usefulness, since a poll is only worth anything if the votes are independent.

SALGANIK, DODDS & WATTS · SCIENCE · 2006

MusicLab: social influence makes outcomes unequal and unpredictable

Participants were randomly assigned either to an independent condition or to one of eight “worlds” where they could see download counts. In the social worlds, success was more unequal (higher Gini coefficient) and harder to predict. The best songs rarely did badly and the worst rarely did well, but almost anything else could happen.

→ DESIGN DECISION

Hide the tally until the person has voted. Each vote then reflects the voter's own view, not the counter's.

MUCHNIK, ARAL & TAYLOR · SCIENCE · 2013

One early upvote measurably inflates the final score

In a five-month randomised experiment on a Reddit-like news site, one artificial upvote raised the chance of the next rating being positive by about 32%, and final ratings ended about 25% higher on average. The effect was asymmetric: people corrected negative manipulation but piled onto positive.

→ DESIGN DECISION

The feed doesn't rank by raw vote count. It shows no counts before you vote, only the number of votes so far, never who is ahead.

LORENZ, RAUHUT, SCHWEITZER & HELBING · PNAS · 2011

Seeing others' answers narrows a crowd without making it more accurate

144 participants made factual estimates, with money at stake for accuracy, and could revise them after seeing others' answers. Information sharing reduced the diversity of answers without improving the group average, while making people more confident.

→ DESIGN DECISION

Arguments are hidden until you vote too, and they're grouped by side, so a reader meets the best case for each option instead of a pile-on.

Trade-off. Hiding results makes browsing less satisfying for someone who only wants to see outcomes. The poll page will offer “Skip and see results”, which records a non-vote, so lurkers are still served and the independence of the data is kept.

04 · Research · choice and ranking

Why two options, and how to rank them

SCHEIBEHENNE, GREIFENEDER & TODD · J. CONSUMER RESEARCH · 2010

Choice overload is weaker than its reputation

A meta-analysis of 63 conditions from 50 experiments (5,036 participants) found a mean choice-overload effect of virtually zero, with large variation between studies. More options tended to help when people already had clear preferences.

→ WHAT I DIDN'T CLAIM

“Fewer choices are always better” isn't supported by the evidence, so it isn't the reason for a binary format. The reason is legibility: with two options, one bar, one margin and one verdict say everything.

HACKER NEWS RANKING · news.arc, ANALYSED BY K. SHIRRIFF

Time decay keeps a feed fresh

The commonly cited form is score = (votes − 1) / (hours + 2)1.8. The exponent, called gravity, controls how fast older items sink. The live site also applies penalty factors, for example to posts with many comments relative to votes.

→ DESIGN DECISION

“Rising” uses votes and arguments from the last 48 hours, divided by (age + 2)1.5. Polls that are close get a small boost, since a near-even split is the most useful one to add a vote to.

E. MILLER · “HOW NOT TO SORT BY AVERAGE RATING” · 2009

Small samples lie

Sorting by a raw percentage ranks 2 out of 2 above 100 out of 101. Miller recommends the lower bound of the Wilson score confidence interval, which takes sample size into account.

→ DESIGN DECISION

Below 10 votes a poll is labelled “Too early to call” and gets no “leaning” label. Argument ranking uses the Wilson lower bound of the share of readers who marked it convincing.

The verdict scale

Too early to calln < 10
Too close to callmargin ≤ 6 pts
Leaning Thisleader below 70%
Landslide · Thatleader at 70% or more

05 · Research · market and visual landscape

What exists, and what to avoid looking like

Precedent · Gas (2022)

Curated polls can spread fast, but at a cost

Gas reached #1 on the US App Store within about three months of launch, and Discord acquired it in January 2023. Its polls were written by the company, not by users, and growth relied on referrals that schools found intrusive. Much of the usage data is the founder's own.

Lesson: well-written prompts drive engagement. The redesign seeds every topic with carefully written questions but keeps user-written ones as the core.

Precedent · Instagram poll stickers

One tap is the lowest-effort form of participation

Instagram has reported that about 60% of businesses posting Stories add an interactive element such as a poll each month. Published “engagement rate” benchmarks for polls mostly come from marketing blogs without sources, so I didn't rely on them.

Lesson: voting must take one tap and give instant feedback. Commenting stays optional and comes after the vote.

Visual research · the “AI-made” look

The design defaults that make an app look generated

Design commentary in 2025–26 describes a cluster of tells: a purple or indigo gradient hero, the Inter typeface as the safe pick, a centred hero followed by three identical rounded cards with icons, and gradients used as background decoration. Several writers trace the purple to Tailwind's default palette, which dominated the tutorials models learned from.

Instead of a gradient
Two flat inks with fixed meanings: denim always means THIS, marigold always means THAT.
Instead of Inter
A condensed scoreboard face for numbers, a newsroom grotesk for text, a mono for counts.
Instead of feature cards
The product opens on the real feed, with a poll you can vote on in the first screen.

Design principles from the research

1. Independence first. Nothing on screen tells you how others voted until you've voted.

2. Honest numbers. Always show n. Never call a result on a small sample.

3. One tap to take part. Voting never needs a page load, a confirmation or an account prompt mid-flow.

4. Close the loop. Askers report their final choice, so voters learn whether their advice was taken.

New features

  • Outcome reports from askers, with notifications to voters
  • Arguments grouped by side, with “convincing” marks
  • Closing times and automatic closing
  • Save, search and topic filters
  • Crowd-alignment stats by topic
  • Edit context before the first vote; delete your own poll
  • Report a poll or argument; asker can turn arguments off

Accessibility as drawn

  • Real buttons, links and labelled inputs throughout
  • Touch targets of at least 44 px
  • THIS and THAT differ in lightness as well as hue, so they stay distinct for colour-blind users and in greyscale
  • Visible focus states; motion respects reduced-motion settings
  • Every percentage is printed as text, never only as bar length

Hardening the engine

The redesign needs a backend that enforces its rules. Each change below answers an audit finding or a category in the OWASP Top 10 (2025). This section is the implementation plan; the build follows sign-off on the design.

Separate votes from argumentsvotes (one per user per poll, enforced by a key) and arguments (many per user, each tagged with a side). A vote is only accepted if the option belongs to the poll and the poll is open.A01 Access Control
